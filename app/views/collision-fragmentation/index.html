{{ explainer("What do \"conjunction\" and \"fragmentation\" mean?", "

Two terms come up a lot on this page:

  • Conjunction: two tracked objects passing close together in orbit. It's what we call a collision risk before it's assessed. MSH tracks these as conjunction-events.
  • Fragmentation: a breakup incident, where an object splits into new trackable debris. MSH tracks these as fragmentation-events.

Most conjunctions are routine wide misses with no computed probability. The list below buckets them by NSpOC's own \"interest\" rating (Low, Medium, High) instead. The \"Requires Analysis\" table further down is different: it uses a dedicated MSH endpoint that only surfaces events past a probability threshold, so each row has a real computed collision probability.
