What the labels mean: Live from MSH API is fetched directly from the Monitor Space Hazards service on this request. No data available means the figure is wired up to live data, but the service didn't respond this time. No live data source connected means there's no live source yet. Either way, we never substitute a placeholder number.

“Collision Risks to UK Satellites” and “Collision Alerts from NSpOC” (both marked \"current snapshot\") reflect the current tracked catalogue: one row per event, most recent revision only. They don't move when you change the month above, which is why the label stays \"current snapshot\".

  • There's no lifetime total available. Requesting the full historical archive times out.
  • Neither figure is filtered to UK-licensed satellites specifically. We checked the full OpenAPI parameter list for every conjunction and satellite endpoint, and no such filter exists yet.

Collision Risks (This Month) is genuinely month-scoped and live, pulled straight from MSH's monthly conjunction-events breakdown, a fast, purpose-built endpoint that stays well under a second even over a 12-month range.

It's a big number because it counts every screened object pair recorded that month, tens of thousands most months. Two things we've ruled out as the cause:

  • Duplicate CDM revisions: we tested a 2,000-event sample and found zero duplicates.
  • A missing UK filter: 91.8% of events already involve a UK-licensed object, so this data is UK-scoped by construction, not global.

It's typically much larger than NSpOC's own reported monthly figure, though monthly volume is volatile too (one month measured 2,357, against 40,000 to 78,000 in the surrounding months), so a fair comparison depends on which month you check. We checked MSH's full API spec directly: no endpoint or filter produces the narrower, analyst-reviewed figure NSpOC actually reports. The closest candidate, a report filter on a different endpoint, returns close to zero rows a month, nowhere near NSpOC's figure either. That's a genuine gap in what MSH exposes today, not something we can query around. We've raised it with the MSH/NSpOC team.

“Collision Events Requiring Analyst Review” is different again: it counts events that have crossed a real computed collision-probability threshold and need an analyst's attention right now. It's a current outstanding-review count, not a historical monthly total.
